Two products

A matrix times its transpose can be taken in two orders. One order compares the rows with one another; the other compares the columns. On the movie ratings the rows are people and the columns are movies.

1. A small matrix, two products

X has one row for each of people and one column for each of genres. A cell holds the number of movies of that genre the person rated. XT, the transpose of X, is X with its rows and columns exchanged. Cell (i, j) of X XT is the dot product of row i and row j of X. Cell (i, j) of XT X is the dot product of column i and column j.

Right of X: X XT, its rows level with the rows of X. Below X: XT X, its columns under the columns of X. A filled cell is coloured by its value, white to blue.

The same matrix, two questions. X XT compares rows against rows: it compares the people by what they rated. XT X compares columns against columns: it compares the genres by who rated them. With n rows and m columns, X XT is n × n and costs n × n × m multiplications; XT X is m × m and costs m × m × n. Here n = and m = : and multiplications. Both products are symmetric: cell (i, j) equals cell (j, i).

One movie, three definitions of similar

The seven movies most similar to the chosen one under each of three matrices, by cosine similarity, on one scale from 0 to 0.9.

One product, three matrices. The cosine similarity of two columns is their dot product divided by the product of their lengths. Divide every column by its length, and all the cosines at once are one product, UT U. All three lists come from that product. What changes is the matrix.

In B a column records only who rated the movie. Popularity and overlap in raters influence these similarities; ratings of a movie do not tell us whether people liked it. With the ratings as given and 0 in the missing cells, a missing rating counts as a rating of zero stars. A missing rating is not a rating of zero. Subtract each person's mean over the kept movies they rated, and then put 0 in the missing cells. Zero represents no deviation from that person's usual rating; it is a modelling choice, not an observed opinion. Two above-mean ratings or two below-mean ratings both add a positive product; opposite deviations add a negative one. Centred similarity therefore measures agreement in deviations, including shared dislikes, and still depends on which ratings are missing.
