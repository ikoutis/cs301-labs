Correlation, in pictures

Two columns of a table make a cloud of points. The correlation coefficient measures the direction and strength of a linear association. For many columns, all the coefficients come from one matrix product.

The data are the MovieLens dataset ml-latest-small of GroupLens Research, University of Minnesota (F. M. Harper and J. A. Konstan, 2015): the 1,297 movies with 20 or more ratings, as the notebook of meeting 14 keeps them. Two columns, one cloud

Each movie is one point. To standardize a column: subtract its mean, divide by its standard deviation.

The correlation coefficient

Hover a point to read it.

The correlation coefficient

r = the average of zx × zy over the rows = cos θ

  • zx and zy: the two columns, standardized.
  • A point above both means, or below both, adds a positive product (orange). Above one mean and below the other: a negative product (blue).
  • r is between −1 and 1.
  • θ is the angle between the two centred columns (each column minus its mean), seen as two vectors with one entry per row.
  • r measures a straight-line relation only: points on a curve can have r near 0.

The correlation matrix

correlation matrix = ZTZ / n = Z.T @ Z / n

  • n is the number of movies. Cell (i, j) is the average of zi × zj.
  • The diagonal is 1: a column has correlation 1 with itself. The matrix is symmetric: cell (j, i) equals cell (i, j).
  • Reordering moves each row together with its column, and changes no number. The order comes from hierarchical clustering: genres with high correlations end up next to each other.
  • A block: genres with positive correlations among them, on the same movies more often than if they were independent.
  • The blocks: Musical, Animation, Children, Fantasy · Adventure, Action, Sci-Fi, IMAX · Crime, Thriller, Mystery, Film-Noir. Drama is negative with Action and with Comedy.
