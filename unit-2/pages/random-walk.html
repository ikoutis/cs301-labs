A walk on a graph

The exact answer is a linear system

Call xv the probability that a walk from node v reaches red first. At red it is 1 and at blue it is 0. From any other node, the first step goes to each neighbour with the same probability, and from there the chance is that neighbour's own number. So every other node's probability is the average of its neighbours' probabilities:

One such equation for each node: sixteen linear equations in sixteen unknowns, A @ x == b, solved by np.linalg.solve with no simulation and no sampling error.

nodeneighbourswalks finishedsimulatedexact

The simulated fractions differ from the exact values by a few hundredths with a few hundred walks; the difference shrinks slowly, like one over the square root of the number of walks. The linear system gives all sixteen numbers at once, to every digit the computer keeps.
