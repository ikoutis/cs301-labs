Correlation

Correlation measures the direction and strength of a linear association. A matrix product computes it for every pair of features.

Scatter plots of movie features

  • Each movie is one point: x from one column of the table, y from another
  • Left: the spread of a movie's ratings against their mean
  • Right: its number of ratings, on a log scale, against the mean
Notes. Two columns of the table, and each movie becomes one point. On the left, the standard deviation of a movie's ratings against their mean: the cloud falls to the right, showing less variation among ratings of movies with high means. The five-star ceiling constrains the spread near that end of the scale. On the right, the number of ratings against the mean: a slight rise. Correlation summarizes the direction and strength of the linear association.

Standardized coordinates

z=x−μσ
  • Standardize each column: subtract its mean, divide by its standard deviation
  • Above both means or below both: a positive product (orange); one of each: negative (blue)
  • r is the average product, between −1 and 1
Notes. Standardize each column: subtract its mean and divide by its standard deviation, so that it has mean zero and standard deviation one. The cloud moves to the origin. Now multiply the two coordinates of each point. A point above both means, or below both, gives a positive product; a point above one mean and below the other, a negative one. The correlation coefficient r is the average of those products: here most points are blue and r is minus 0.41. It is always between minus one and one, and it measures a straight-line relation only; points on a curve can have r near zero.

Correlation as a dot product

r=1n∑i=1nzx,izy,i=zx·zyn=cosθ
def correlation(x, y):
    return standardize(x) @ standardize(y) / len(x)

r of spread and mean: -0.413 as a dot product, -0.413 by np.corrcoef
  • A sum of products over n: a dot product divided by n
  • θ: the angle between the two centred columns, seen as vectors with one entry per movie
Notes. The average of products is a sum of products divided by n, and a sum of products is a dot product. So r is one line of NumPy: the dot product of the two standardized columns over n. np.corrcoef computes the same number. The second reading: the two centred columns are two vectors with 1,297 entries each, and r is the cosine of the angle between them, which is why r cannot leave minus one to one. Act 3 draws that angle. The standard deviation here divides by n, as np.std does; then the average of the products is exactly the r of np.corrcoef.

Genre correlations

C=1nZTZ,Cij=1n∑k=1nZkiZkj
Z = (G - G.mean(axis=0)) / G.std(axis=0)   # every genre column standardized
C = Z.T @ Z / len(Z)                       # the correlation matrix, 19 by 19
assert np.allclose(C, np.corrcoef(G, rowvar=False))

Z is 1,297 by 19; Z.T @ Z / n is 19 by 19: 1,297 multiplications for each of its 361 cells
  • Cell (i, j): the dot product of columns i and j, over n
  • 171 distinct pairs of genres, excluding self-comparisons
  • 1 on the diagonal; symmetric
Notes. For the 19 genre columns, standardize every column of G into Z and form Z transposed times Z over n. Cell i, j is the dot product of standardized columns i and j over n: their correlation. The whole matrix of 171 pairs comes from one product, with no loop over the pairs; each of its 361 cells is 1,297 multiplications. The diagonal is one, a column with itself, and the matrix is symmetric. The check against np.corrcoef holds to round-off. G has 19 columns because the genre that no kept movie has was dropped: a constant column has standard deviation zero and cannot be standardized.
