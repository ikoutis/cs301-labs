Part 2 of 4 · Meeting 16

Seeing the SVD

The singular values describe the cloud’s spread along its axes.

The SVD of centered data Seeing the SVD

Subtract the mean of each column:

X=A−1μ⊤

μ: column means; 1: column of ones.

X=USV⊤

  • V: perpendicular unit axes.
  • S: singular valuesσ1≥⋯≥σm≥0
  • US=XV: coordinates along those axes.
  • Spread along axis k: σk/n

Here n≥m. U has perpendicular unit columns.

The axes in two dimensions Seeing the SVD

  • The SVD of the 200×2 matrix gives two axes, v1 and v2, at right angles.
  • Standard deviation along axis k: σk/n.
  • The condition number compares the spreads:κ=σ1/σ2
  • Here σ1 stays fixed. As σ2 decreases, κ grows and the cloud approaches a line.

The axes in three dimensions Seeing the SVD

  • The SVD of the 240×3 matrix gives three perpendicular axes.
  • The condition number is largest spread divided by smallest:κ=σ1/σ3
  • View along v3: plane. View along v2: thickness.
  • As σ3 shrinks, the cloud approaches a plane and κ grows.

Exact rank and approximate dimension Seeing the SVD

Exact dimension: count the nonzero singular values.

Approximate dimension: keep enough coordinates for the required accuracy.

κ=σ1/σm

A large condition number means at least one direction is thin relative to the widest.

Compare all the singular values to decide how many coordinates to keep.

Finding the points of view

How can we find these axes from the data?

How much variance is there along a direction?

  • Choose a unit direction w: ‖w‖=1.
  • A point x has coordinate x·w along w.
  • Xw contains all n coordinates.
  • X is centered: these coordinates have mean 0.
  • Their variance is the average square:‖Xw‖2/n

Rotate w to find the smallest variance.

Find the direction with the least variance

For centered data and a unit direction w

Variance along w = ‖Xw‖2n

Write the variance using the covariance matrix

C=X⊤Xn

‖Xw‖2n=w⊤Cw

The first direction we find

Minimize w⊤Cw,   ‖w‖=1

Call the minimizing direction vm.

Small variance means little spread along w.

Projection removes the component (x·w)w from each point.

The average squared movement is exactly the variance along w.

Keep the space perpendicular to the direction with least variance.

In three dimensions, this means keeping a plane.

The least-variance direction in 3D

Use the same idea in 3D. Compare the variance along different directions.

The least-variance direction is v3.

Remove each point’s v3 component.

x^=x−(x·v3)v3

The remaining points lie in the plane perpendicular to v3.

What remains is a plane

The component along v3 is gone. All points now lie in one plane.

Viewed face-on, this is a 2D dataset.

Two axes at right angles describe positions within the plane.

Q2=[q1q2]
Y2=XQ2

Each row of Y2 contains the two coordinates of one point.

Repeat inside the plane

Y2 is now a 2D dataset.

C2=Y2⊤Y2/n

Find its unit direction a2 of least variance, then keep the perpendicular line.

v2=Q2a2

This expresses the removed direction in the original 3D coordinates.

The remaining direction is v1. This projection discards variation within the original plane.

The same construction in m dimensions

Qd stores the current axes in the original coordinates:Yd=XQd

Find a unit direction ad minimizing ‖Yda‖2/n.

vd=Qdad

Choose perpendicular unit columns for B, spanning ad⊥.

Yd−1=YdB   Qd−1=QdB

Start with Qm=I. Stop at d=2.
The remaining axis is v1.

Account for all the variation What the axes preserve

Squared distance from the mean (now 0):‖xi‖2=xi12+xi22+xi32

Total variance: average squared distance.(1/n)∑i=1n‖xi‖2

Sum C’s diagonal, or use the SVD:(σ12+σ22+σ32)/n

Variance along each axis What the axes preserve

Pythagoras for perpendicular axes:

‖x‖2=(x·v1)2+(x·v2)2+(x·v3)2

Average over all points for total variance:(σ12+σ22+σ32)/n

Axis k’s variance: σk2/n.

For a thin cloud, the first two axes account for nearly all the variance.

Data with many coordinates What the axes preserve

  • The reduced SVD gives r=min(n,m) perpendicular axes and r singular values.
  • The variances σk2/n sum to the total variance.
  • If the variances decrease rapidly, a few axes capture most of the variation.
  • Keeping the leading principal directions gives a smaller set of coordinates.

SVD and approximation

The SVD describes variation and helps us choose fewer coordinates.

To remember

  1. For centered data, X=USV⊤ gives perpendicular axes ordered by variance.
  2. The variance along axis k is σk2/n. These contributions sum to the total variance.
  3. Keeping the leading axes reduces the number of coordinates. The discarded variances sum to the mean squared reconstruction error.
