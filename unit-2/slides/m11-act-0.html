Vectorization: a calculation without a loop

def standardize_array_loops(X):
    n, m = X.shape
    Z = np.empty((n, m))
    for j in range(m):
        total = 0.0
        for i in range(n):
            total += X[i, j]
        mean = total / n
        total = 0.0
        for i in range(n):
            total += (X[i, j] - mean) ** 2
        std = math.sqrt(total / n)
        for i in range(n):
            Z[i, j] = (X[i, j] - mean) / std
    return Z

def standardize_vectorized(X):
    return (X - X.mean(axis=0)) / X.std(axis=0)
  • The same arithmetic, two ways
  • One line: the loop runs inside NumPy, in compiled code
  • Loops over a NumPy array: the slowest of all
Notes. One minute. The chart is dataset 7's; every group measured its own in meeting 10. In the one-line version the loop over the numbers runs inside NumPy, in compiled code: tens of times faster than the loops in Python, and a Python loop over a NumPy array is the slowest of all, because each read builds a new Python object for one number.

Broadcasting: a shape that mathematics would refuse

mu = X.mean(axis=0)                      # one mean for each column: m numbers
sigma = X.std(axis=0)
Z = (X - mu) / sigma                     # standardization, in one line
print("shape of X:", X.shape, "  shape of mu:", mu.shape, "  shape of X - mu:", (X - mu).shape)
v = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0])
print("v + 10 =", v + 10, "  one number, added to every entry")

shape of X: (41757, 11)   shape of mu: (11,)   shape of X - mu: (41757, 11)
v + 10 = [11. 12. 13. 14.]   one number, added to every entry
  • X - mu: (n, m) minus (m,)
  • Mathematics: write the means on every row first, 1μT
  • NumPy: broadcasting, with no copies stored
  • Today: the bias of a layer, + b
Notes. The one idea of meeting 10 that this meeting uses on every slide. X - mu subtracts m numbers from an array with n rows and m columns; in mathematics the shapes do not fit, and the m means would have to be written on every row first. NumPy does that for you, without storing n copies. The rule: shapes are compared from the right, and a dimension that is missing, or is 1, is repeated; v + 10 repeats one number for every entry. The bias of a layer is added in the same way.

  • N, the number of points, is the first dimension; the point's own dimensions follow
Notes. A batch adds one dimension at the front, the number of the point, and the dimensions after it are the point's own: m for a day, H by W by 3 for an image, L by m for a text, padded with rows of zeros to one length L. The images of the batch are 96 by 96, and the one image shown is one of them. Every operation of meeting 10 ran on a whole batch at once: X - mu on all the rows, the grey rule on thirty images, U @ v on 50,000 words. The days of Newark airport are the data of Act 1.
