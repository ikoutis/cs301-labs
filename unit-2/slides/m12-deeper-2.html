What Computing Costs

A deeper look: the seconds of this meeting in joules and in dollars, and a model trained at scale.

Three costs of one computation

time=operationsoperations per secondenergy=power×timemoney=price per hour×hours
  • Power in watts: joules each second
  • 1 kilowatt-hour = 3.6 million joules
  • The first cost is the count and the rate of this meeting; the other two follow from it
Notes. Every computation of the meeting has three costs. Its time is the count divided by the rate. Its energy is the power the processor draws, in watts, joules each second, times the time. Its price is what an hour of the processor costs, times the hours. Electricity is sold by the kilowatt-hour, 3.6 million joules.

What decides the cost

cost≈count×seconds for one operation×price of a second
  • The count: O( ), the same on every machine
  • The seconds for one operation: the machine, the order, the memory
  • The price of a second: watts, and dollars an hour
  • A faster processor can cost more an hour and less per operation
Notes. The whole meeting in one line. The count is what Recap 1 went over and O( ) describes, the same on every machine. The seconds for one operation are what Recap 2 and the first deeper look measured: the interpreter, the order of reading, the cache, the processor. The price of a second is this deck: the watts of the processor and what an hour of it costs. Whether a GPU costs less per operation depends on both its rental price and the throughput achieved by the workload.
