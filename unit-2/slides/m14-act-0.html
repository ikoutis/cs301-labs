  • R: 610 rows, 1,297 columns, NaN where there is no rating
  • B = ~np.isnan(R): True where there is one; 8.6% of the cells
  • A missing rating is not zero stars
Notes. R is the matrix: one row for each person, one column for each kept movie, the stars in the cell, and NaN, not a number, where the person did not rate the movie. B records where the ratings are. Only 8.6 percent of the cells hold a rating, even among the most-rated movies. A missing rating records no observed opinion. Movie features from column sums

countj=∑iBij=(1TB)j,meanj=(1TR0)jcountj
ones = np.ones(len(people))
count = ones @ B                           # number of observed ratings per movie
R0 = np.nan_to_num(R)                     # zero-fill for summing observed ratings
mean = (ones @ R0) / count
  • A feature of a movie: one number computed from its column
  • A column sum is a product with a vector of ones
  • Divide each sum by its observed rating count
Notes. A feature of a movie is one number that describes it: the number of its ratings, their mean, their spread, the year in its title. The number of ratings of every movie is the sum of each column of B, and a column sum is a product with a vector of ones: ones @ B multiplies every row by one and adds the rows. For the mean, nan_to_num puts 0 in the holes so that the column sums are the sums of the ratings, and the division by the count leaves the zeros out. This computes the means for all movies without an explicit Python loop. R0 denotes the zero-filled matrix in the formula.

Mean rating by genre

mean per genre=GTmeanGT1
G: 1,297 movies by 19 genres, 1 where the movie has the genre
mean_per_genre = (G.T @ mean) / (G.T @ ones_movies)
  • G.T @ mean sums movie means; G.T @ ones_movies counts movies in each genre
  • Each movie counts once in each of its genres
Notes. Each movie has one or more genres. G is the genre matrix: a row for each movie, a column for each genre, 1 where the movie has the genre. G transposed times the vector of means adds up, genre by genre, the mean ratings of the movies that have it; G transposed times a vector of ones counts them. Their ratio is a mean of movie means, with equal weight for each movie. In pandas, expanding the genre lists and grouping by genre gives this calculation. Film-Noir is rated highest on average, Horror lowest.
