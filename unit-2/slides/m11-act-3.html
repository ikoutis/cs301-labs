O(nKm)

  • The time grows as the number of passes does: a factor near 2 when n doubles, near 8 when all three double. The time of one pass is nearly the same in the three rows: time ≈ (time of one pass) × n × K × m.
  • Computer science writes O(nKm), read "of the order of n K m": the notation keeps how the count grows with the sizes, and drops the constant in front, the time of one pass.
  • For a layer with n = m = K the count is n³: double the size, and the time is multiplied by 8. That is the factor of Act 2 between the sizes 2,048 and 4,096.
  • The page What matrix operations cost lists the count for every operation of the meeting. A transpose and a reshape cost nothing: no number is moved.

The same operations, in other seconds

Long arrays against short arrays: memory-bound

  • The two rows run the same additions, by the same code. Only the place of the numbers differs.
  • A processor has a small, fast memory of its own, the cache. The short arrays fit into it and stay there.
  • The long arrays do not fit: each number is fetched from the main memory, used in one addition, and the result is sent back. The processor spends most of its time waiting for numbers.
  • Such a computation is memory-bound: the memory sets its speed, not the arithmetic.

The page CPU and GPU, parts 3 and 4: one product divided among the cores, and the two memories.

The product: compute-bound

  • In X @ W.T a number is not used once: each entry of X is multiplied with a weight of every neuron.
  • NumPy's product works on blocks of the two matrices that fit into the cache, and uses each number many times while it is there. The processor spends its time on arithmetic: compute-bound. Every core helps, and so do the instructions that handle several numbers at once.
  • The GPU: thousands of small cores on the same product. This pays for compute-bound work on large arrays, and, as Act 2 measured, not for small ones.
  • O( ) is the right first estimate of a cost. The chart shows what it leaves out: how the program is run, where the numbers are, and which processor does the work.
